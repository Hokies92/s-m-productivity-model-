# Workstream 5 — Capture and develop lessons

## Purpose and boundary

Extract Scott's accumulated experience and turn it into clear, testable proposals for a more powerful commercial model. Capture can begin immediately; changes to original model behavior are evaluated and versioned through the project process.

## Accountable roles

Scott: author of lessons and business priorities. Research/analysis lead: structured interviews, evidence and drafts. Coordinator: decision tracking and affected-stream review. Roles besides Scott are unassigned.

## Inputs

Scott's explanations, original workbook and supporting material, `docs/MODEL.md`, existing project records, current workstream questions and relevant evidence Scott supplies.

## Work plan

1. Capture one lesson at a time in Scott's words: the business situation, what he observed, the underlying relationship and why it matters.
2. Link each lesson to a source or example; distinguish Scott's confirmed intent, observed outcomes, inference and open hypotheses. Avoid turning historical context into unverified competitor or causal claims.
3. Identify whether the lesson explains original functionality, improves entry/explanation, or proposes new inputs, rules, scenarios, costs or outputs.
4. For a proposed enhancement, document expected user value, affected workstreams, required information, compatibility with original cases, evidence needed, implementation costs and an acceptance example.
5. Prioritize with Scott after the Gate 1 inventory. Preserve important ideas in the register even when their implementation is deferred.
6. After Gate 1 acceptance, develop selected proposals into approved versioned changes, tested against reference cases and connected interfaces.

## Deliverables and handoffs

- A searchable lesson register and decisions/questions for Scott, linked to source evidence and dates.
- Concise proposed enhancements with model and integration impact, provided to streams 1–4.
- An ordered evolution backlog showing selected, deferred, unverified and completed items.

## Milestones and completion

First: initial lessons and a shortlist of interview questions. Gate 1 contribution: all material clarification needed to explain the original is handed to stream 1; evolution ideas are captured without changing parity criteria. After the gate: each selected enhancement has explicit owner, rationale, interface effects and acceptance evidence.

## Dependencies and controls

Stream 1 determines whether a lesson describes original intent; streams 2–4 assess design and integration. Do not promise dates, causal performance improvements or exclusivity without supporting evidence. Scott decides priorities.

## First actions

Begin with a structured discussion of one original design decision—what problem it solved, what choices it enabled and what later experience would change. Record questions and evidence before proposing a new formula.
